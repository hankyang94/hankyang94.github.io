Linear Conic Optimization (Conic-LP)

Primal\[\min_x\ \ip{c}{x}\qquad Ax=b,\quad x\in\KK.\]
Dual\[\max_{y,s}\ \ip{b}{y}\qquad A^*y+s=c,\quad s\in\KK^*.\]

Problem data: \(\theta=(A,b,c)\).
For SDP, write \(x=X\), \(s=S\), \(c=C\) and use the Frobenius inner product.

Linear Program (LP)

\(\KK=\R_+^n\)

Second-Order Cone Program (SOCP)

\(\KK=\{(t,u):t\geq \|u \|_2\}\)

Semidefinite Program (SDP)

\(\KK=\Sn_+^n\)

KKT Solutions and Strict Complementarity

Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) solution set

\[ \operatorname{KKT}(\theta):=\left\{(x,y,s):\quad \begin{array}{ll} Ax=b,\quad x\in\KK & \qquad\text{(primal feasibility)}\\[3pt] A^*y+s=c,\quad s\in\KK^* & \qquad\text{(dual feasibility)}\\[3pt] \ip{x}{s}=0 & \qquad\text{(complementarity)} \end{array}\right\}. \]

Standing assumptions: \(\operatorname{KKT}(\theta)\ne\varnothing\) and \(A\) is surjective (\(AA^*\succ0\)).

Strictly complementary (SC) solutions for SDP

\[\operatorname{KKT}_{\mathrm{SC}}(\theta):=\bigl\{(X,y,S)\in\operatorname{KKT}(\theta):X+S\succ0\bigr\}.\]

For a KKT pair,

\[\boxed{X+S\succ0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rank X+\rank S=n.}\]

First-Order Methods

We consider a family of primal–dual splitting methods, including:

ADMM: Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers
\[\begin{aligned}y^{k+1}&=(AA^*)^{-1}\!\bigl[A(c-s^k)+\sigma^{-1}(b-Ax^k)\bigr],\\s^{k+1}&=\Pi_{\KK^*}\!\bigl(c-A^*y^{k+1}-\sigma^{-1}x^k\bigr),\\x^{k+1}&=x^k+\sigma\bigl(A^*y^{k+1}+s^{k+1}-c\bigr).\end{aligned}\]
\(\sigma>0\).
PDHG: Primal–Dual Hybrid Gradient
\[\begin{aligned}x^{k+1}&=\Pi_{\KK}\!\left[x^k-\eta_x(c-A^*y^k)\right],\\y^{k+1}&=y^k+\eta_y\!\left[b-A(2x^{k+1}-x^k)\right].\end{aligned}\]
\(\eta_x,\eta_y>0\), \(\eta_x\eta_y\norm{A}_{\rm op}^2<1\).
\(\Pi_{\mathcal C}\): orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal C\).
  • Under mild conditions, they converge to a KKT solution.
  • Easy to implement: linear algebra and cone projections.
  • For SDP with large \(n\), the bottleneck is projection onto the PSD cone \(\Sn_+^n\).

Normalized KKT residuals at \((x^k,y^k,s^k)\), with \(x^k\in\KK\) and \(s^k\in\KK^*\):

Primal infeasibility\[r_{\mathrm p}^{(k)}:=\frac{\norm{Ax^k-b}}{1+\norm b}.\]
Dual infeasibility\[r_{\mathrm d}^{(k)}:=\frac{\norm{A^*y^k+s^k-c}}{1+\norm c}.\]
Relative duality gap\[r_{\mathrm g}^{(k)}:=\frac{|\ip{c}{x^k}-\ip{b}{y^k}|}{1+|\ip{c}{x^k}|+|\ip{b}{y^k}|}.\]

\(\boxed{r_{\max}^{(k)}:=\max\{r_{\mathrm p}^{(k)},r_{\mathrm d}^{(k)},r_{\mathrm g}^{(k)}\}}\).

First-Order Methods as Fixed-Point Iterations

Abstract the algorithm into a fixed-point iteration:

\[\boxed{z^{k+1}=T_\theta(z^k),\qquad z^k\in\HH.}\]

\(\theta=(A,b,c)\) is the problem data; \(z\) is the algorithm’s fixed-point state.

The fixed-point set encodes KKT solutions:

\[\ZZ_\theta:=\Fix T_\theta=\bigl\{z\in\HH:T_\theta(z)=z\bigr\}.\]

Concrete example: ADMM on SDP, with matrix state \(Z\in\Sn^n\):

\[T_\theta(Z)=Z-P\bigl(\Pi_+(Z)-X_b\bigr)-P^\perp\bigl(\Pi_-(Z)+\sigma C\bigr).\]

\(P=A^*(AA^*)^{-1}A\), \(P^\perp=\Id-P\), \(X_b=A^*(AA^*)^{-1}b\),
\(\Pi_+=\Pi_{\Sn_+^n}\), \(\Pi_-=\Id-\Pi_+\).

\[\ZZ_\theta=\bigl\{X-\sigma S:(X,y,S)\in\operatorname{KKT}(\theta)\bigr\}.\]

Convergence Dynamics of First-Order Methods

Classical theory and the usual perception
Worst-case ergodic gap: \(O(1/k)\) .
Max KKT residual drops fast for a few thousand iterations, then plateaus. We lose patience and stop.
The sublinear-rate picture seems to fit.

ADMM on SDPs reveals at least three macroscopic regimes:

Fast linear convergence
Persistent slow progress
Several plateaus, then fast

\(r_{\max}^{(k)}\): maximum KKT residual.　\(\norm{Z^{k+1}-Z^k}_F\): fixed-point residual.

Convergence varies across the state space: fast in some regions, stuck in others.

Residual Field

A dynamic algorithm becomes a static field:

\[z^{k+1}=T_\theta(z^k)\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad\boxed{\delta_\theta(z):=T_\theta(z)-z.}\]

Study the spatial variation of \(\delta_\theta\); its zero set is \(\ZZ_\theta=\Fix T_\theta=\{z:\delta_\theta(z)=0\}\).

Example 1: ADMM on an LP (\(\sigma=1\))

\[\min_{x\geq0}\ x_1\qquad\text{s.t. }x_1-x_2=0.\]

State \(z=x-s\in\R^2\); fixed-point segment:

\[\ZZ_\theta=\{(y-1,-y):0\leq y\leq1\}.\]

Arrows indicate field direction.

Background color encodes field strength \(\norm{\delta_\theta(z)}_2\).

The green segment is the zero set.

Residual Field

A dynamic algorithm becomes a static field:

\[z^{k+1}=T_\theta(z^k)\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad\boxed{\delta_\theta(z):=T_\theta(z)-z.}\]

Study the spatial variation of \(\delta_\theta\); its zero set is \(\ZZ_\theta=\Fix T_\theta=\{z:\delta_\theta(z)=0\}\).

Example 2: ADMM on an SDP (\(\sigma=1\)) \[\min_{X\in\Sn^2}\ 0\qquad\text{s.t. }\operatorname{tr}X=1,\quad X\succeq0.\]

State \(Z=X-S\in\Sn^2\), with coordinates

\[Z=\frac12\begin{bmatrix}1+w+u&v\\v&1+w-u\end{bmatrix}.\]

The fixed-point set is a disk:

\[\ZZ_\theta=\{Z\succeq0:\operatorname{tr}Z=1\},\qquad w=0,\ u^2+v^2\leq1.\]

3D arrows: field direction.
Arrow color: field strength \(\norm{\delta_\theta(Z)}_F\).

Region-Wise Sharpness

Sharpness modulus over a region \(\region\subseteq\HH\):

\[\boxed{\kappa_\theta(\region):=\inf_{z\in\region\setminus\ZZ_\theta}\frac{\red{\norm{\delta_\theta(z)}_Q}}{\blue{\dist_Q(z,\ZZ_\theta)}}.}\]

Field strength (backward error) divided by distance to the fixed-point set (forward error).

Both norm and distance use the algorithm’s metric \(Q\succ0\), with \( \|u\|_Q=\sqrt{\ip{u}{Qu}}\); for ADMM, \(Q=\Id\).

Averagedness Connects Sharpness to Convergence

ADMM, PDHG, and sGS-ADMM are \(\alpha\)-averaged maps with \(\alpha=\tfrac12\):

\[T_\theta=(1-\alpha)\Id+\alpha N,\qquad 0<\alpha<1,\quad N\text{ nonexpansive in } \|\cdot \|_Q.\]

Lemma (Positive Sharpness Gives One-Step Contraction)

Let \(\ZZ_\theta\ne\varnothing\) and \(\boxed{\blue{0<\kappa:=\kappa_\theta(\region)<\infty}}\). Define \(q:=\sqrt{1-\tfrac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\kappa^2}\in[0,1)\). For every \(z\in\region\),

\[\begin{aligned}\boxed{\dist_Q(T_\theta z,\ZZ_\theta)^2}&\leq\dist_Q(z,\ZZ_\theta)^2-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\norm{\delta_\theta(z)}_Q^2&&\text{(definition of $\alpha$-averagedness)}\\[5pt]&\leq\boxed{q^2\dist_Q(z,\ZZ_\theta)^2}&&\text{(positive sharpness).}\end{aligned}\]

If \(z^0,\ldots,z^{k-1}\in\region\), then

\[\boxed{\dist_Q(z^k,\ZZ_\theta)\leq q^k\dist_Q(z^0,\ZZ_\theta).}\]

Central question: where in the state space is the residual field sharp?
